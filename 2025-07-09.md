## Fellowship

### Fellows

A new application to become an NHS-R Community Fellow has been submitted to the NHS-R Community inbox. 
It was queried whether the application would require review from the Abstract Peer Review Committee. 
It was requested that confirmation be provided as soon as possible whether an independent member is needed to review the application.

It was noted that there has been limited interest in the fellowship. 
This could be due to multiple factors such as reduced availability to contribute to the community, being unaware that a community member qualifies to become a Fellow and/or that the main contributors to the community are already Fellows. 
It was advised that the fellowship could be promoted at the conference, with an opportunity for attendees register their interest.

It was queried whether the benefit of being awarded the honorary title outweighed the cost. 
It was suggested that a blog made up of short case studies of current Fellows, detailing their experiences obtaining the title, may encourage other community members to apply. 
Concerns were raised that it may not be the correct climate to chase community members with the current impending changes to the NHS. 
The Fellow title is not an accredited title however may be beneficial to add to your CV to help show continued contribution to the community.

### Use Of AI
It was acknowledged that there is little awareness of bias, training methods, and limitations when using ChatGPT, AI, Copilot and other LLMs. 
Noting that there a significant number of colleagues within the NHS are using/trialling Copilot to assist with tasks (not limited to administrative tasks). 

It was advised that the following colleagues have an in depth knowledge and understanding of AI/LLMs and would be good to contact: 
•	Dan Schofield
•	Harriet Sands 
•	Sam Hollis
•	Adam Hollis.
It would also be beneficial to contact the AI Ambassadors network (NHS-wide) to request some training resources and guidance.  
Chris Mainey volunteered to provide a simple neural networks session.

Some analysts use AI to write code, make their code more efficient and/or request guidance on how to build a model. 
It was confirmed that ChatGPT has previously been utilised to write unit tests for the NHSRwaitinglist package.
The tool was efficient at testing the inputs and outputs, and expected errors. 

It was queried whether the NHS-R Community could host a webinar of caution regarding AI to help raise awareness of how to use the tools safely and inline with NHS regulations.
